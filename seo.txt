Tiger Safaris in India — SEO Foundation Strategy
Executive summary
The site wins search by owning five parks in depth (zones, permits, lodges, timing) rather than chasing broad "India tour" terms. The foundation has three non-negotiables:
1. Every page must ship as static HTML.
2. A Stays and Safari Booking layer must be added under each park.
3. The URL structure must be locked before Google indexes the current one.
Decisions locked for this plan
Decision	Choice	SEO consequence
Booking model	Enquiry-led (form + WhatsApp → quote)	Schema uses TouristTrip / TravelAgency, no live Offer prices. Conversion pages are "Book Ranthambore safari" style landing pages that hand off to an enquiry.
Hotel side	Large inventory, OTA-style	Biggest risk in the plan. Hotel pages need an indexation gate (section 9) or they become thin duplicates of Booking.com / MakeMyTrip listings.
Domains	Single domain, no EMDs	All authority consolidates on tigersafarisinindia.com. Park-specific tools replace microsites (section 18).
Markets	Indian + international travellers	Two intent sets: domestic ("Corbett safari booking", Hindi-English queries, weekend trips from Delhi) and international ("best tiger safari India", tours, luxury lodges). One English site, en-IN primary.
Top 5 P0 actions (first 2 weeks)
1. Prerender every route. Use vite-react-ssg, or migrate to Astro or Next.js. Today a crawler sees only the title and one hero line (section 2).
2. 301 redirect www → non-www. Enforce a single trailing-slash rule.
3. Lock the final URL map (section 17). Add /stays/ and /safari-booking/ under each park before launch indexing.
4. Build the five park hubs to full depth first, then layer in hotels and blog content.
5. Verify the domain in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the sitemaps, and ship baseline schema (Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList).
1. Strategic positioning — why this works
No one owns the planning layer for Indian tiger safaris. That layer joins permits, zones, lodges and transfers into one decision, and it is the gap this site fills. Each of today's players covers only one piece:
* Government forest portals sell permits.
* OTAs sell rooms.
* Local agents run thin single-park sites.
Why this works
* Narrow scope builds authority faster. Five parks is a small enough topic to cover exhaustively. Google rewards sites that answer every question in a niche over sites that skim many.
* High-consideration purchase = many searches before booking. A tiger safari trip involves:
    * choosing a park and a season
    * choosing a zone and a vehicle
    * choosing a lodge and transfers
* Each of those is a separate search. That is a long trail of informational queries you can own before the enquiry.
* Specifics beat generic tour copy. These are questions OTAs and portals don't answer. Each answer is a ranking asset:
    * Which zone has the best sighting record?
    * How far is the lodge from the gate?
    * Jeep or canter for a family of five?
* AI answer engines quote structured facts. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews cite pages with clear tables (zone, gate, distance, fees, season) and explicit FAQs. Building that way earns both classic rankings and AI citations.
* Hotels are the revenue multiplier, not the SEO lead. Rank on park and safari intent first. Then route that traffic into stays via internal links, rather than fighting OTAs head-on for "hotels in Sawai Madhopur".

Direction changes
* From "inspiration site" to "decision site." Every park page must answer, in visible HTML tables:
    * zones
    * gates
    * safari timings
    * vehicle types
    * season / closure months
    * how far each stay is from the gate
* From destinations-first to park-entity-first URLs. Short /ranthambore/ hubs with children beat /destinations/ranthambore-tiger-safari/. "Tiger safari" is already in the domain, so repeating it in every slug adds length, not relevance.
* Lock structure now. The site is two days old, so URL changes cost almost nothing today. After indexing, every change needs redirects and costs ranking time.
* Spelling variants. Use the dominant spelling in URLs (Ranthambore, Jim Corbett). Mention variants once in the body copy ("also spelled Ranthambhore"). The live page already does this.

4. Keyword strategy — principles
Keywords follow the formula park × intent modifier. Each URL gets one primary keyword, and priority is set by intent and business value before volume. The master sheet (sections 11–15) uses the columns: Keyword → Search Intent → Content Pillar → Priority → Proposed URL → Content Required → Status.
Rules
1. One primary keyword per URL. Two pages chasing "Ranthambore safari booking" split ranking signals (cannibalisation). Secondary variants go on the same page as H2s and in FAQs.
2. Intent decides the page type, not the keyword's wording.
    * Informational ("best time to visit Corbett") → guide.
    * Commercial investigation ("best hotels near Ranthambore") → curated list.
    * Transactional ("Ranthambore safari booking") → booking landing page with an enquiry form.
    * Before assigning a page type, check what Google already ranks for the query and match that format.
3. Priority bands:
    * P0 = transactional, park-level money terms (safari booking, hotels/resorts, tour packages). Build these first.
    * P1 = planning terms that feed P0 (zones, best time, cost, how to reach, comparisons).
    * P2 = long-tail, editorial and Discover content (tiger profiles, sightings, packing lists).
4. The modifier matrix generates the sheet. Apply each modifier to each of the five parks: safari booking · online booking · zones · timings · safari price / cost / fees · best time · hotels · resorts · near [gate] · luxury · budget · tour package · from Delhi / Jaipur / Mumbai · 2-day / 3-day itinerary · jeep vs canter.
5. Two audiences, two vocabularies.
    * Domestic queries are park + logistics heavy ("Corbett weekend package from Delhi", "Ranthambore canter booking").
    * International queries are country-level and experience-led ("best tiger safari in India", "India tiger tour 10 days", "luxury tiger lodges India").
    * Guides and tours carry the international terms; park hubs carry the domestic ones.
6. Don't target "official booking" navigational queries. People searching "Ranthambore official safari booking site" want the government portal.
    * Ranking a private site for those queries draws pogo-sticking and complaints, and it risks looking like impersonation.
    * Instead, target "how to book Ranthambore safari" with an honest guide. Name the official portal, then explain what you add: zone strategy, lodge, transfers, naturalist.
7. Spelling and transliteration variants (Ranthambhore, Jim Corbet, Hinglish queries) are handled on-page, not as separate URLs. Hindi-language pages are a P2 test after the English site ranks.
8. Plan around the season. Core zones close in the monsoon and reopen in October (verify exact dates per park and zone each year).
    * Refresh park, best-time and booking pages in August–September, before demand returns.
    * Change the "Updated" date only when the content genuinely changes.
9. Volumes are added, not assumed. The sheet in this doc is prioritised by intent and business value only — it contains no search volumes. Before final prioritisation, add Volume and Difficulty columns from Google Keyword Planner and Ahrefs or Semrush. After launch, re-rank using real Search Console impressions.
5. Content standards & E-E-A-T
The site ranks on first-hand field knowledge. Every guide carries:
* a named naturalist as author
* original photos
* dated facts that link to the official source
Trust is the signal that matters most for a site taking travel money. Show it on every page.
E-E-A-T, made concrete for this site
Signal	What it means here	How to show it
Experience	You have actually done these safaris	Trip reports with date, zone, gate and what was seen; original photos (keep EXIF and the originals); "Field notes" boxes on park pages
Expertise	Your people know the parks	/naturalists/[name]/ author pages: bio, parks covered, years guiding, photo portfolio, sameAs links (Instagram, LinkedIn); author + reviewer byline on every guide
Authoritativeness	Others vouch for you	Lodge partnerships, conservation NGO mentions, press coverage, photographer features (section 19)
Trust	Safe to hand over a trip budget	Legal entity name, address, phone and WhatsApp on every page; clear "what's included" + cancellation policy; real guest reviews linked to Google Business Profile; a plain statement that you are a private operator, not the forest department; HTTPS; privacy policy
Google systems to build for
* Core ranking (helpfulness). People-first content written for the traveller is now judged inside Google's core ranking systems. A page that only restates what every OTA says will not hold rankings.
* Spam policies.
    * Scaled content abuse is the big risk for a large hotel inventory: hundreds of near-identical, auto-written hotel pages can demote the whole site.
    * Rule: AI may draft, but a human verifies every fact and adds the site-specific data before a page goes live.
* Reviews system. "Best hotels near Ranthambore" and comparison pages are judged as reviews. Show:
    * first-hand evidence (you stayed, inspected or sent guests)
    * pros and cons
    * how you chose
* Discover. Two requirements apply: the page must be indexed, and it must meet Discover's content policies.
    * No clickbait titles. No withholding the key fact to force a click.
    * Timely, photo-led stories do best.
* AI Overviews and answer engines. The answer comes in the first 40–60 words under each H2. Facts go in tables. Every claim is attributed to an official source or to your own data.

Hard rules
* Never invent sightings, tiger names, fees or dates.
* Fees and timings change. State them with "as of [date]" and link the official forest department page.
* Tiger profiles use the forest department's T-number IDs and show the date of last sighting info. Retire or update profiles when a tiger dies or disperses.
* Every hotel claim ("300 m from Zone 6 gate") must be verified by your team or the property, and logged in the hotel data sheet with a date.
6. Internal linking rules
Every page links in three directions: up to its hub, forward one funnel step, and sideways to related knowledge. No page may be an orphan, and every money page sits within three clicks of the home page.
A. Hub-and-spoke (authority flows to the park hub)
* The park hub links to all of its spokes: safari booking, zones, hotels hub, best time, how to reach, safari cost, tour packages.
* Every spoke links back to the hub within its first 150 words, plus a breadcrumb.
* The hotels hub links to every indexable hotel page for that park with plain crawlable <a href> links, paginated as /page/2/. Filters may use JavaScript, but the hotel list itself must be in the HTML.
* /tiger-reserves/ links to all five hubs. The home page links to all five hubs and to the /plan-your-safari/ page.
B. Connect the user journey (always one step forward)
From	Must link to	Example anchor
Guide (best time, which park)	Relevant park hubs	"Ranthambore tiger safari"
Park hub	Safari booking + hotels hub	"book a Ranthambore safari", "hotels near Ranthambore"
Zones guide	Safari booking (same park)	"book Zone 3 to 5 safaris"
Safari booking	Hotels hub + tour packages + enquiry	"stay close to the gate"
Hotel page	Safari booking (same park), 3 nearby hotels, zones guide, enquiry	"plan safaris from this lodge"
Hotel list	Each listed hotel + safari booking	hotel names
Tour (multi-park)	Each park hub on the route + hotels used	park names
Journal story	Park hub + relevant zone/tiger profile + enquiry	"see tigers in Ranthambore"
Every page also carries the persistent Plan your safari button (header) and an in-content enquiry block after the main answer.
C. Connect knowledge clusters (sideways)
* Comparisons ("Ranthambore vs Corbett") link to both hubs and both safari-booking pages.
* Safari types link to the parks that offer them: boat → Panna, Satpura; walking → Satpura; canter → Ranthambore, Corbett. Each park hub links back to the safari types available there.
* Season cluster: the India-wide best-time guide ↔ each park's /best-time/ page ↔ each park's safari booking page.
* Logistics cluster: the how-to-reach pages link to tours that start in the same city (Delhi → Corbett weekend; Jaipur → Ranthambore / Sariska).
* Wildlife cluster: tiger profiles and species pages link to their park and zone, and to each other.
Mechanics
Rule	Standard
Anchor text	Descriptive and varied. Use the exact-match keyword once per linking page at most. Never "click here".
Contextual links	3–10 meaningful links inside body copy per page; navigation and footer links don't count
Breadcrumbs	On every page except the home page: Home › Ranthambore › Hotels › [Hotel] — with BreadcrumbList schema (section 7)

No links to noindex/redirects	Internal links point to final 200 URLs only. Never link to redirects or noindex filter pages.
Audit	Monthly Screaming Frog crawl: orphans, click depth > 3, broken links, links to redirects
7. Structured data (schema) plan
Schema here is for entity clarity (Google, AI answer engines), not for chasing rich results. Ship it as one JSON-LD @graph per page, rendered in the static HTML. Every value in the markup must also be visible on the page.
Note on FAQs: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results for all sites on 7 May 2026. FAQPage markup is still valid and can stay as a semantic aid, but it will not earn a SERP feature (SEJ, May 2026).

Do not mark up
* AggregateRating / Review about your own company: self-serving reviews are ineligible and risk a manual action.

9. Technical SEO foundation checklist
Tick every P0 box before the site is promoted. Three items carry most of the risk on this stack (React + Vite on Vercel):
* static rendering
* soft-404s from SPA rewrites
* the hotel indexation gate
Rendering & crawlability (P0)
* Every indexable route is static-generated at build time; curl -s URL shows the full H1, body copy, tables, FAQs and internal links
* Unknown URLs return a real 404 status, not the SPA shell with 200. Vercel catch-all rewrites cause soft-404s, so add a 404 route that returns the 404 status code.
* Internal links are <a href> in the static HTML
* robots.txt allows everything public, blocks only /thank-you/, internal search and filter query parameters, and lists every sitemap
* No important content behind tabs or accordions that load only on click (content in collapsed accordions is fine if it is in the HTML)
URLs, redirects & canonicals (P0)

10. Image SEO & the Google Discover playbook
Original wildlife photography is the site's visual identity and its Discover ticket. Every article gets a large, well-cropped, credited hero image, and max-image-preview:large stays on sitewide (already live).
Google's current Discover image guidance asks for images that are:
* at least 1200 px wide
* at least 300K pixels
* 16:9
* declared via og:image or schema
* not logos or text graphics
(Search Engine Land, Mar 2026)
Image & technical requirements
* Large images on every article and park page: hero at least 1200 px wide (export 1600 px), 16:9 crop with the subject inside the safe area. max-image-preview:large robots meta sitewide — required for large Discover cards.
* Declare the preferred image: a per-page og:image plus primaryImageOfPage or image in the Article / WebPage schema. Never the logo, never a text-on-image graphic.
* Descriptive, keyword-aware file names. For example: ranthambore-zone-3-tigress-padam-talao-lake.webp, not IMG_4021.jpg. Pattern: park-zone-subject-place.
* Natural-language alt text on every image. Describe the image, don't list keywords. For example: "Tigress walking along the shore of Padam Talao lake in Ranthambore National Park at dawn". Decorative images get alt="".
* Compress and serve modern formats:
    * Squoosh / TinyPNG for one-offs; sharp in the build for everything else.
    * WebP with AVIF where supported, plus a srcset of 480 / 960 / 1600 px.
    * Lazy-load below the fold; never lazy-load the hero.
* Image XML sitemap (/sitemap-images.xml) listing each page's key images. Use only <image:loc>: Google dropped the caption, title, geo and licence tags in 2022, so licensing goes in ImageObject schema instead (section 7).
* Credit and licensing: a visible photographer credit on the page, and IPTC creator / copyright embedded in the file.
* Strip GPS EXIF from every wildlife photo before upload. Publishing precise tiger locations is an anti-poaching red flag and can harm your standing with forest departments.
* Original, high-resolution photography only for wildlife and lodges. Licensed lodge photos are acceptable with credit; no stock tigers.
Editorial requirements (Discover)
* Titles say what the story is.
    * Good: "First tiger sightings as Ranthambore reopens for the 2026–27 season".
    * Bad: "You won't believe what we saw…". Discover demotes clickbait and withheld-information titles.
* Timely beats evergreen in the feed. Build a seasonal calendar:
    * park reopening (October)
    * winter fog safaris
    * summer waterhole season
    * monsoon closure and buffer-zone safaris
    * officially announced cubs or relocations (only after the forest department confirms them)
* Photo-led formats work best: sighting reports, "tigers of Zone X" profiles, photo essays, a naturalist's field diary, behind-the-scenes of a safari morning.
* Every story has a named author (naturalist) and photographer, a published date, and an updated date when edited.
* No AI-generated or composited wildlife images presented as real. Manipulated media breaches Discover's content policies and destroys trust. If AI art is ever used (illustrations, maps), label it clearly.
* Cadence: 2–4 journal stories per month in season, 1–2 in the monsoon.
* Provide an RSS/Atom feed for /journal/, linked in <head>, so Chrome's Follow feature and feed readers can subscribe.
* Measure in the GSC Discover report. A story that earns Discover traffic gets a follow-up within 2 weeks.

18. EMD strategy & destination tools
Decision: one domain, no exact-match microsites. Park-specific tools on the main site do the job EMD microsites used to do, and they build authority in one place instead of five.
Why not EMDs
* Google removed the ranking boost for low-quality exact-match domains back in 2012. Today an EMD ranks only on its own content and links.
* Five park microsites would split links, reviews and brand searches across six domains. Each would start from zero authority.
* Thin single-park sites with templated copy look like doorway pages, which is a spam-policy risk.
* Booking trust is brand-driven. Travellers handing over a trip budget search the brand, not the park domain.
Note: tigersafarisinindia.com is already a partial-match domain for "tiger safari in india". That helps relevance and brand recall.
* The flip side: branded links will naturally carry keyword anchors. Keep the rest of your anchor profile diverse (URLs, "click here"-style naturals, naturalist names) so it doesn't look over-optimised (section 19).
* Optional brand protection, not SEO: register obvious typo variants (for example tigersafariinindia.com) and 301 them to the main domain. Never put content on them.
Destination tools — what replaces microsites
